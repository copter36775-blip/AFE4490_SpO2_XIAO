Headroom
—

PRPCOUNT bottoms out at 800, which puts the chip's ceiling at 4901 Hz — above that the 50 µs conversion and the 50 µs sample window stop fitting inside a quarter of the period, and the ADC returns a number it never finished making. Measured on this board: USB streams the whole range clean, 4901 Hz at 63 kB/s; Bluetooth to 750 Hz at 9 kB/s. Above 2000 Hz the firmware switches to a compact frame — four channels at 22 bits instead of six at 32, which is lossless — so 3000 Hz actually costs less bandwidth than 2000. Past what the link carries, raise Decimation; the AFE keeps converting at the full rate either way.

Bluetooth only. Chrome's radio goes quiet for around two seconds at a time, so the display reads out of a buffer to keep the sweep moving. A deeper buffer never pauses and sits further behind your finger; lag in the status bar says where it actually is. USB uses 90 ms and ignores this.

Only those seven clocks exist on the nRF52 SPIM, and the BSP rounds a request up to the next one — SPIM1 also caps at 8 MHz. The BSP forces high drive on SCK/MOSI, which overshoots on dupont wire and couples into MISO; standard drive is usually the bigger win. Measure the ceiling with Sweep SPI clock in the Diag tab.

Display

Each trace gets its own auto-scaled lane by default. RED and IR sit at very different DC levels, so a shared axis compresses the pulsatile part of both into a flat line — that is why the raw plethysmograph looks featureless when they are overlaid.

The AFE's LED-minus-ambient registers come out inverted compared with the waveforms in most publications (datasheet notes on figures 128 and 129), and physically more blood means more absorption and so less photocurrent. Both push systole downward, so the display flips it back.

Stored configuration

no link

The AFE4490 has no non-volatile memory — all 49 registers come back at their datasheet reset values the moment power is removed, and there is nothing on the chip to write a setting into. Save to board therefore stores the configuration in the nRF52's own flash: 39 registers plus PRF, duty, decimation, stream format, SPI clock and drive strength. Boot applies the SpO₂ preset first and then replays the profile over it, so a setting added by a later firmware still starts from something sane.

SpO₂ default below resets the chip and leaves the stored profile alone, so the next power-up brings your settings back. Forget & factory is the one that erases it. Reboot restarts the board the way a power cycle would, which is the only honest way to check that a profile really persists.

Presets

SpO₂ default rewrites the whole register map to the normal pulse-oximetry configuration, and Reset AFE pulses the chip's own reset first. Neither touches the display; Reset view to defaults in the Display section above does that, and nothing else. Auto gain walks LED current and transimpedance until the DC level sits near half of full scale — the loop from datasheet figure 135.

What to fix now

Read from the live registers and the last status line, so it only says things that are true right now. Nothing here changes SpO₂ accuracy — that is the calibration curve in Diag. Everything here changes how much noise sits on top.

Presets

Each one writes a full Tx and Rx configuration and then runs auto-gain, so the emitters land where that situation wants them. They are starting points measured on this board, not certified settings — check the ADC headroom card afterwards and confirm both channels arrived.

SpO₂ calibration

SpO2 = 110.00 − 25.00·R
reference-design default, not measured on this hardware

110−25R is a population average for somebody else's emitters and somebody else's probe geometry. Set the simulator to a saturation, type it in, and Capture averages R over the dwell — the spread it reports is this instrument's precision at that point, which is a different question from whether the curve is right. Two points define the line; a third is what tells you the line was the right shape, so watch the residuals rather than the coefficients. This characterises the instrument against that simulator — it is a statement about the pair, not a clinical calibration against blood.

Battery & charger

—V
no link

The 22-bit converter is calibrated; this one is not. VBAT arrives through a 1 M / 510 k divider read against the internal band-gap reference, so the absolute figure carries the tolerance of two resistors and a reference. Measure the cell with a meter, type the volts here, and the firmware trims to match — Reset puts it back to the nominal divider.

Charge current is left as the board ships it. A 200 mAh cell takes 100 mA at 0.5 C or 50 mA at 0.25 C; the slower one runs cooler.

Sensor & cable diagnostics

Emitter-off drives both LED currents to zero for a few seconds and measures what the photodiode still sees. Anything pulsatile that survives is not ours: room light, mains flicker, or a simulator with an active emitter of its own. It restores the currents afterwards, and the numbers it prints are millivolts at the TIA output, so they compare directly with the raw traces.

The AFE walks nine fault cases — LED open/short, photodiode open/short, and each input shorted to the ground or LED cable. It takes 8 ms and the ADC data is invalid while it runs.
